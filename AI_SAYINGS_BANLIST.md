# AI sayings ban list

**Use this as an editing rule, not an AI detector.** A flagged word is a reason to review a sentence, not evidence of who wrote it. Keep the word when it is the precise, natural choice, appears in a quotation or title, or is required by a technical meaning.

## Hard ban: stock filler and scene-setting

Delete or rewrite these when they add no specific meaning:

- “It is important to note that…” / “It is worth noting that…”
- “In today's ever-evolving … landscape” / “In the realm of …”
- “At the end of the day…” / “When it comes to…”
- “Let's delve into…” / “Let's take a deep dive into…”
- “Here's the thing:” / “Here's why:” / “What this means is:”
- “In conclusion…” as a generic sign-off; state the conclusion itself.
- “This is not just X; it is Y” and “It's not about X, it's about Y” when used as a prefab dramatic turn. Keep a contrast when the distinction is real and useful.
- “A testament to…” / “a rich tapestry of…” / “a vibrant … landscape” as ornamental praise.

## Rewrite-on-sight vocabulary

Unless literal, quoted, or the best exact term, replace these with a concrete verb or noun:

**delve, delve into, intricate, intricacies, underscore, showcasing, realm, tapestry, testament, groundbreaking, garnered, boasts, advancements, aligns**

Examples: “delve into the issue” → “examine the issue”; “serves as a testament to” → say what it proves; “underscores the importance” → state the implication; “showcases” → “shows” or name what is on display.

This short set leans on measured lexical evidence. It is intentionally not a ban on ordinary connective words such as *however*, *also*, or *therefore*.

## Pattern warnings: revise when clustered

- Paragraphs that all begin with “Moreover,” “Furthermore,” “Additionally,” or “In addition.”
- Repeated “three-part” lists that sound balanced but do not add distinct information.
- Paired opposites in every section (“not only X, but also Y”; “from X to Y”).
- Generic claims followed by a three-item list and a summary that repeats the claim.
- Empty intensifiers: **crucial, pivotal, vital, robust, seamless, transformative, innovative**. Replace with the actual consequence, measurement, or property.

These are review signals, not single-word prohibitions. Research finds that generated text can favor formulaic transitions and more uniform vocabulary, but human writing uses the same devices. Judge repetition in context.

## Editing check

1. Search for the hard-ban phrases and rewrite each occurrence.
2. Search for the vocabulary list; keep only cases that are precise, literal, or part of a quotation.
3. Read the first sentence of each paragraph aloud. Vary the starts and remove transitions that merely announce a connection.
4. Replace broad praise with the specific action, result, number, example, or limitation.
5. Keep the writer's natural register. Do not swap a clear everyday word for a “human-sounding” synonym just to evade a list.

## Research basis

- Juzek and Ward's [COLING 2025 paper](https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-main.426/) identified 21 words whose recent rise in scientific abstracts and overuse in ChatGPT-3.5 abstracts overlapped. The paper's focal set includes **delve, intricate, underscore, showcasing, realm, garnered, boasts, groundbreaking, advancements**, and related forms. The findings are about scientific abstracts and an earlier model, not a universal rule for every genre or current model.
- Matsui's [2025 PubMed study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12679996/) compared 135 previously reported AI-associated terms with common medical phrases across PubMed records (2000–2024). It found increases in many flagged terms, including **delve, underscore, meticulous, boast, showcase, intricate, tapestry**, while noting that some trends began before ChatGPT and may have been amplified rather than created by LLMs.
- Muñoz-Ortiz, Gómez-Rodríguez, and Vilares' [comparative news-text study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10462-024-10903-2) found greater vocabulary and sentence-length variation in human text than in six tested LLMs. Its controlled news setup does not establish a universal “AI style.”

**Practical rule:** ban empty templates; flag a small evidence-backed word set; judge everything else by repetition, specificity, and voice.
